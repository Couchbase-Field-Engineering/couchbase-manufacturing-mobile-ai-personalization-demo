Chapter 1 · A day on the floor

A production plan that doesn't wait for Wi-Fi.

Every night an AI model reads historical sales, market trends and supply chain data and works out what each line should build, how much inventory each product should carry and which materials to order. The planner who has to act on it does so on the shop floor, next to the line - which is exactly where a phone doesn't work.

Press shops

A steel building full of 400-ton presses. The plant Wi-Fi was engineered for scanners at the dock doors, not for the middle of the press line.

Foundry and machining bays

Castings stage between furnaces and CNC cells. Handhelds drop off the network every time a crane passes.

Paint and coating booths

Shielded, sealed and grounded. Nothing gets in or out, including LTE.

Mezzanine stores

The tool crib and the kitting racks sit on a steel deck above the floor - a dead zone by design.

Cold docks and yards

Receiving happens past the dock doors, where coverage stops and the late truck is.

The walk between them

A planner reviews two dozen products a day with supervisors, at the line. Back at the desk is too late to change today's sequence.

Friday, 8:30 am - HP-2200 Hydraulic Gear Pump, Line 3 · CNC Cell B, Peabody MA

Maya Chen is standing in the foundry bay with the cell supervisor. Two alerts are open: distributor orders are running 38% over forecast, and the castings PO is six days late. The app already knows all of this - the demand-planning scores landed on her phone at 5 am, with a production plan: build 1,200 pumps this week instead of the 900 scheduled, because cover is 4.2 days against a 10-day target, and order six materials. Four pallets of castings and the rest come to $18,970, which the app tells her is $3,774 over what's left of the product's quarterly material budget, and that the warehouse only has three of the four pallets. She walks the readiness checklist with the supervisor and releases it as a budget override with a reason. The phone says On device. Twenty minutes later, at the cell door, it says Awaiting approval - and by the time she reaches the SMT line, Production order PRO-261113-… released: 1,200 units, both alerts cleared, one pallet of castings on order. She never had signal at the line and never needed it.

Talking points. Production planning is an offline problem first and an AI problem second. The forecast, the schedule, the inventory position and the material plan are on the phone before the planner reaches the line; nothing on the floor waits on a network. The AI assistant and the governance come along for free once the data plane is right.
Chapter 2 · What the app needs

Three decisions from the model. Eight requirements to act on them.

Factory schedules

How much to build this week

The 8-week forecast from sales history, the order book and market signals becomes a recommended build quantity, checked against the line's capacity. Over capacity means an overtime shift the Plant Manager approves.

Inventory levels

How much to carry

Days of finished-goods cover against the service class's target, and a recommended safety stock that moves with forecast error and demand trend.

Material ordering

What to order, and when

Days-to-stockout per material from usage and open POs, supplier lead times, commodity indexes for forward buys - checked against warehouse stock and the material budget.

On the phone
  • Every product in the plant, on device. Forecast, inventory, this week's schedule, alerts, material cover, budget, order history and the model's production plan - before the planner reaches the line.
  • Schedule and materials in one production order, with no signal. Build quantity, readiness checklist, material costing, the capacity check and the budget check all compute locally.
  • Sync when the network comes back. Production orders go up; releases, materials on order and approvals come down, to the same card the planner tapped.
  • One source of truth per plant. Two planners can't claim the same last pallet of castings; a product has one schedule, one budget and one alert list.
For the AI - and the data platform
  • Grounded in today's export, and only in this product's profile - never another product's, never another plant's.
  • Repeat questions are cheap. The second planner to ask "how is safety stock set?" costs nothing; the same product question twice costs nothing.
  • Actions are governed. Production orders, budget overrides and overtime shifts run as audited tool calls; overrides wait for the Plant Controller and overtime waits for the Plant Manager.
  • The Databricks bill doesn't grow per tap. One scheduled job a day. No warehouse query per screen, no Model Serving call per product.
Talking points. The last requirement is the one the data platform team cares about. Databricks is excellent at producing the forecast and the plan from years of sales, supplier and market data. It is an expensive place to serve them from, 150,000 times a day, to phones that are half the time offline.
